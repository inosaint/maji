Category research

What design-leadership sites actually do

Three comparable sites, read for structure rather than style — all belong to senior design leaders selling judgement rather than craft output. The pattern is remarkably consistent, and it is not the pattern of an IC portfolio.

juliezhuo.com

Julie Zhuo

Opens with the name, then a vertical list of roles — founder, author, essayist — before anything else. Work is a plain linked list of writing and talks; no thumbnails, no grid, no hero image at all.

Taken: state role and scope in the first screen as plain text. Left: a pure link list is too thin here — Sukmaji's evidence is metric-based and needs the numbers visible.

petermerholz.com

Peter Merholz

Single column, generous whitespace, hierarchy carried almost entirely by type size. Leads with a one-sentence statement of what he does for organizations. Contact appears twice — in the nav and again in the footer.

Taken: the one-sentence proposition as the first thing on paper, and contact in both nav and footer. The org-effectiveness framing is very close to Sukmaji's own.

danmall.com

Dan Mall

Front-loads credibility hard: named executives, follower counts, "hundreds of articles since 2005." Work is presented as flagship ideas rather than client case studies.

Taken: a numbers band early — but sourced from business outcomes and headcount, not vanity metrics. Left: testimonials and CTAs stacked toward a product funnel; wrong register for structural candour.

Structural decisions

How the research changed the build

  1. Blue hero, then paper. The hero reproduces the cover — right-hand axis, huge italic wordmark, the strike line, the highlighter rule, the quiet email. Then the page drops to #FBFBF6 so 1,500 words of argument stay readable.
  2. Proposition before credentials. Following Merholz: one sentence in Crimson Pro at display size — "I build the collaboration structures that let siloed product groups ship one coherent experience" — with the marker on the operative phrase.
  3. Scope as a numbers band, not prose. 7 / 16 / 2 / 4 set as large numerals, with the counterpart map underneath as running text. This is the single fastest thing an executive reader needs.
  4. Four entries, each on its own page. The homepage carries image + title + description per entry; the full Impact → Role → What's interesting lives on work-*.html. Prev/next pagers link them into a ring.
  5. Principles & Philosophy is entry 04, cited as design leadership — sitting beside the case studies rather than under them. Track record and the 2026 theme moved onto that page, which leaves the homepage lean: hero, proposition, scope, four cards, contact.
  6. Diagrams promoted over screens. The service blueprint, the paired customer/driver flow and the ads audit swimlane are the hero images. Entry 04 has no diagram, so it gets a typographic plate built in CSS rather than a faked screenshot.
  7. No section nav. Removed at review — the header is now just the wordmark and one Contact button. Five anchor links on a short page was nav theatre.
  8. Hero re-composed. Wordmark top-left, the descriptor/rule/email cluster anchored bottom-right — a diagonal rather than a single right-hand stack.
  9. Mobile. Breakpoints at 1000px and 760px; hero cluster re-rags left; the pager stacks. With the nav gone there is no hamburger to get wrong.
