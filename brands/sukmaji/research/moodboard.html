Stage 2 — Moodboard & Directions

Three ways to
build the frame

You supplied one reference: page 1 of the Portfolio 2026 deck. Everything below is derived from it. Colour is anchored — the four values are sampled straight out of that page, not invented. Type is open — the deck's own humanist italic appears as exactly one option among nine, and it has to win on merit. Tone keyword from Stage 1: structural candour.

The reference

What the cover actually does

  • A flat, unmodulated field. No gradient, no texture, no photograph, no shadow. The blue is a wall, not a background — which means the whole design rests on typography and interval.
  • One hard right-hand axis. The wordmark's right edge, the yellow-green rule's right edge and the email's right edge all land on the same vertical. Everything hangs from that line; the left side is left deliberately ragged.
  • Roughly 60% of the page is empty. The confidence is in the restraint. Any direction that fills the space with content or decoration loses the source's whole character.
  • The strikethrough is the idea, not an effect. Architectural Industrial Interface Designer — the struck words are tinted back to ~50%, the survivor is full white. This is a person editing his own definition in public. It is the single most reusable device in the deck.
  • The highlighter rule appears once, alone. Solid #E3EB8F, long and thin (~1:85), floating in the lower right. On inner pages the same colour becomes a literal marker-pen highlight behind headings and impact numbers. So it has two jobs: rule and emphasis — the system should keep both.
  • The email is quiet, not loud. Set in the tinted lavender, not white. Contact is present but refuses to shout — worth preserving rather than converting into a big CTA button.

Anchored — not up for debate

DIRECTION 01

The Blue Field

Maximum fidelity to the reference. The site is the cover, unrolled — one continuous electric-blue wall, white type hanging off a right-hand axis, the highlighter rule doing the section-breaking. Case studies arrive as white "cards" cut into the blue, which is exactly what pages 5–7 of the deck already do. Risk: sustained saturated blue is demanding over a long scroll; it needs generous intervals to stay comfortable.

The real break. Drops the serif entirely. Syne's widened, slightly wrong-looking forms read as contemporary art-institution rather than consultancy — it makes "Architectural Industrial" literal. Hanken Grotesk keeps long case-study prose readable underneath.

Imagery & texture

Nothing photographic. The reference has no images at all, and the honest extension of that is to let the product artefacts you already own be the only pictures: the current-flow/proposed-flow service blueprint, the queue screen from Terminal 3, the GoFood homepage. They sit on white plates floating in the blue, with a 1px lavender hairline, no drop shadow, no device mockup frames.

Texture comes from interval, not material. Very large vertical gaps between sections — 160px+ on desktop — so the blue is allowed to be empty in the same proportion the cover is. The only recurring graphic element is the yellow-green rule, reused at three fixed widths.

Explicitly avoided: gradients, mesh blobs, glass blur, noise overlays, floating 3D shapes, stock office photography, phone-in-hand renders. None of it exists in the source and all of it would violate structural candour.

DIRECTION 02

Marked-Up

Takes the strikethrough and the highlighter — not the blue — as the real idea, and inverts the ground. Warm paper, near-black ink, blue demoted to a link-and-label colour, and the yellow-green used as an actual marker pen: behind impact numbers, behind case-study titles, exactly as pages 5–7 of your deck already use it. The site reads as a working document someone has been editing, which is a very direct expression of "the organization's maturity is my deliverable." Risk: gives up the instant recognisability of the blue wall — the blue survives only in flashes.

Inverted — sans display, serif body

Sukmaji Kumoro

Architectural Industrial Interface Designer

Structurally the opposite move. Headlines get the sans; the long-form argument gets the serif. Reads less like a designer's portfolio and more like a policy paper — which, given ethics and governance are your actual subject matter, may be the more honest costume.

Imagery & texture

The marker pen is the texture. Highlight spans sit slightly off-register — a couple of pixels wider than the word, baseline nudged down — so they read as applied by hand rather than as a CSS background. Struck words keep a real 1.5px rule at mid-x-height, never a fake underline.

Artefacts shown as documents, not products. The service blueprint is the star here: reproduced large, full-width, on paper, with the annotations legible. Screens get a thin #DEDED2 border and a caption in lavender, like plates in a report.

Explicitly avoided: paper-grain images, coffee stains, skeuomorphic torn edges, handwriting fonts. The "document" quality comes from structure, margin and rules — not from pretending to be printed.

DIRECTION 03

Load-Bearing

Takes the struck words Architectural Industrial at face value and builds the frame in public: a visible column grid, hairline rules that actually meet, technical labels, and metrics set in mono. Blue stops being a field and becomes the structure lines. This is the only direction that resolves as a genuine light + dark pair — off-white "drawing" mode and dark "screen" mode — which, per the brief, would be built as one page that themes, with a footer toggle. Risk: can tip into engineering-dashboard if the display face isn't given enough presence.

03·C

Faustina + Chivo + DM Mono

Humanist serif on a technical grid

Sukmaji Kumoro

Architectural Industrial Interface Designer

The warm-blooded version. Keeps the deck's italic as the human voice and lets the grid, rules and mono supply the rigour around it. The friendliest way into this direction, and the easiest to reconcile with Direction 01 if you want to blend.

Imagery & texture

The grid is the ornament. A 12-column rule set stays faintly visible at section boundaries — columns that actually align with the content, not a decorative overlay. Corners meet properly; rules terminate at the grid, never mid-air. Section numbers sit in the margin as 01 / 02 / 03.

Diagrams get promoted to hero. Your current-flow/proposed-flow blueprint is redrawn in the palette — blue strokes, lavender secondary paths, highlighter on the changed nodes — so it stops being a screenshot and becomes brand material. Impact figures are set as big mono numerals against the grid.

Explicitly avoided: blueprint-paper backgrounds, faux-CAD crosshairs, terminal windows, monospace body copy, "scanning" animations. The rigour has to come from real alignment, or the direction is fancy dress.

My recommendation, and what I need from you

Direction 02 “Marked-Up” with pairing 02·B (Crimson Pro + Epilogue), borrowing one thing from Direction 01: keep a full-bleed #4346EB hero band at the top so the site still opens on the blue wall, then drop to paper for the argument. Reason: your portfolio's substance is long-form — thesis, governance, ethical constraints — and paper carries 2,000 words of that far better than a saturated field does, while the highlighter and strikethrough keep the identity unmistakable.

If you'd rather stay closest to the deck, take Direction 01 with 01·A. If you want the site to feel like a step forward rather than a translation, Direction 03 with 03·C — and that's the one that gets a real light/dark toggle.

Tell me: one direction, one type pairing. You can mix — “02's palette, 03's mono for numbers, 01·C's display face” is a legitimate answer. Stage 3 then researches comparable design-leadership portfolios and builds the full responsive homepage from your real content.
