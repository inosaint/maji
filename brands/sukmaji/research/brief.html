The brief

Positioning statement

Sukmaji Kumoro is a design leader whose deliverable is not a screen but an organization — someone who builds the collaboration structures that let large, historically siloed product groups ship one coherent experience.

Over ten-plus years at Gojek he has founded or rebuilt five design organizations — Midtrans, Merchants Platform, Ads & Promo, GoFood, and now Mobility — and the pattern across all of them is the same: he treats the seams between teams as the real design surface. Airport dispatch failed because consumer flow and driver flow were owned by different groups. Ads risked abuse because nobody had written down what the platform would refuse to do. In each case the artifact he shipped was a working model of collaboration, and the metric moved as a consequence.

The site should therefore read as the portfolio of an infrastructure thinker, not a UI showcase. Case studies lead with the structural problem and the ethical constraint; the screens are evidence, not the argument.

Primary audience

Executive hiring principals — founders, CPOs, and heads of product at scaled consumer platforms (SEA-first, but not SEA-only) evaluating someone for VP/Head of Design. They read fast, distrust polish, and want to know within one screen what he is accountable for and what moved.

They need: scope in headcount and business units, named cross-functional counterparts, and outcome numbers with dates attached.

Secondary audiences

Senior designers weighing a team. The Operating Thesis is the recruiting document — ethics as foundation, competing viewpoints as a requirement, "the organization's maturity is my deliverable." Give it real estate, not a footer.

Peers and the wider design community — conference programmers, podcast hosts, people who encounter him through the career-ladder and DLS work. They arrive for one idea and should leave with a way to reach him.

Tone keyword

Structural candour

Structural — the arc of his own self-description (architectural industrial interface designer) is a person who keeps rebuilding the frame. The site should feel load-bearing: visible grid, honest rules, sections that hold weight rather than float.

Candour — he names failure metrics ("public disruption"), refusals ("we don't abuse ads inventories for short-term gains"), and unglamorous work (procedures, operations flow). No superlatives, no hero-shot mystique.

Discipline for every later stage: if a visual move is decorative rather than structural, or a line of copy claims more than the portfolio actually says, it fails the keyword and gets cut.

What the site must carry

  • Scope, stated plainly. 7 product groups, 16 designers and UX writers, 2 managers, and the counterpart map — 7 VP/Head of Product, 6 Head of Business, 2 Head of Marketing, 3 Head of Engineering, plus the VP of Regions & Operations.
  • Three case studies, structured identically. Airport Experience (2023–25), GoRide Instant (2024), Ads Principles (2023) — each as Impact / Role / What's interesting, because that repetition is itself the argument that he works one consistent way.
  • The Operating Thesis as a first-class section, not an About footnote.
  • A forward-looking close. The 2026 theme — an anxiety-free order-to-drop-off experience — makes the portfolio a plan rather than a résumé.
  • One contact route: sukmaji@kumoro.com. Nothing else competes with it.
