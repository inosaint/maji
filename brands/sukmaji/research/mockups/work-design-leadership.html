Operating thesis

Principles & philosophy

Three beliefs that decide how I hire, how I run reviews, and what I will refuse to ship. They are the reason the case studies all look structurally alike.

I

Design should be founded on ethics. Designers thrive when their participation in solving a problem comes from an internal moral drive — and when their cross-functional peers share that drive.

In practice: ethical constraints get written into the brief, not bolted on at review. Ads Principles is that belief with a governance diagram attached.

II

Cultivate diversity and welcome competing points of view. I want my team to challenge each other, debate the fundamentals, delve into the meta, test hypotheses, diverge and converge, and ultimately establish shared principles.

In practice: disagreement is scheduled, not tolerated after the fact — the “high-friction collaboration models” below are the mechanism.

III

Leading a design team is as much about shaping minds as it is about shaping products.

In practice: I judge myself on the artefacts listed under contributions, not on any single launch.

“The organization’s maturity is my deliverable.”

What’s next

2026

Last year my focus was to overhaul collaboration processes across historically siloed product groups. That reshaped how Gojek thinks about supply & demand — centring commitment to the platform, and holding one strategy from OKR definition through to tactical execution. It shipped as cancellation mitigation, loyalty revamps, and pickup experiences.

The theme for 2026 is to leverage those gains to arrive at an anxiety-free order-to-drop-off experience — which eventually paves the way for Gojek to be every Indonesian’s on-demand personal assistant.
